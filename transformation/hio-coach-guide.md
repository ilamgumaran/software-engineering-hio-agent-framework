# HIO Coach Guide

## Overview

Internal HIO coaches are the connective tissue of the transformation. Selected from the pioneer group and broader org during Phase 2, coaches enable cognitive units to form, operate, and grow without depending on external facilitation. This is a **part-time role** — coaches remain active contributors within their own cognitive unit.

---

## Coach Selection Criteria

Select **3-4 coaches** based on these qualities (all are required, not pick-and-choose):

- **Natural facilitator** — people gravitate toward their meetings because they run well
- **High emotional intelligence** — can read a room, sense tension, and name what others feel
- **Respected by peers** — credibility comes from competence, not just enthusiasm
- **Genuinely excited about HIO** — not performing excitement, actually curious and energized
- **Comfortable with ambiguity** — can hold space when things aren't working without panicking
- **Willing to learn in public** — will share their own mistakes and growth edges openly

Avoid selecting people who are primarily motivated by career advancement, authority, or being seen as special. Coaching in HIO is about enabling others, not leading them.

## Five Core Coach Skills

### 1. Facilitate Purpose Workshops

- Guide a new cognitive unit from "group of people" to "unit with shared purpose"
- Help the unit articulate WHY they exist — not just WHAT they build
- Ensure every voice is heard, not just the loudest or most senior
- Connect unit purpose to the broader platform mission

### 2. Recognize Identity Grip

- Spot the signs: rigid role language, resistance to function rotation, defensiveness about expertise
- Name it without judgment — "I notice some attachment to the current way" not "you're being resistant"
- Normalize it — everyone experiences identity grip, including coaches
- Know when to push gently and when to back off entirely
- See [emergence-detection.md](emergence-detection.md) for identity grip patterns

### 3. Design Human-AI Workflows

- Help individuals find their productive pairing with AI agents
- Experiment with different human function + AI agent combinations
- Identify when AI is being underutilized (human doing AI-suitable work) or misapplied (AI doing work that needs human judgment)
- Reference agent guides in [../agents/](../agents/) for pairing recommendations

### 4. Run Harmonization Retrospectives

- Facilitate retrospectives that surface real issues, not performative positivity
- Use the **Harmonization Retrospective** format (see [../workflows/harmonization-retrospective.md](../workflows/harmonization-retrospective.md))
- Ensure retrospective actions actually happen — follow up in the next sprint
- Create psychological safety for honest feedback about the transformation itself

### 5. Read Fulfillment Signals

- Go beyond the Harmony Pulse numbers — watch for behavioral signals
- Signs of high fulfillment: energy in ceremonies, voluntary collaboration, people trying new functions
- Signs of low fulfillment: silence in ceremonies, minimal effort, avoidance of new practices
- Know when to intervene with 1:1 conversations and when to let the team self-correct

## Training Progression

| Stage | Duration | Activities | Milestone |
|-------|----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Shadow** | 1 week | Observe experienced facilitator running all ceremonies, take notes, debrief after each session | Can describe what happened and why in each ceremony |
| **Co-facilitate** | 1 week | Run portions of ceremonies with experienced facilitator present, receive real-time feedback | Successfully facilitated at least one full ceremony segment |
| **Solo facilitate** | 1 week | Run complete ceremonies independently with observer providing written feedback | Positive feedback from unit members on facilitation quality |
| **Coach coaches** | Ongoing | Mentor newer coaches, share patterns across units, contribute to playbook | Another coach credits your mentorship in their development |

## What Coaches Do Each Sprint

### Sprint Kickoff
- Facilitate the full kickoff ceremony for their assigned unit(s)
- Ensure **Analysis Partner** pre-analysis is reviewed and discussed
- Help the unit balance delivery goals with learning goals

### During the Sprint
- **1:1 check-ins** on fulfillment with each unit member (15 min, informal)
- Observe **Daily Harmony Checks** — not to lead them, but to notice patterns
- Flag identity grip patterns early and address them gently
- Propose small **workflow experiments** — "what if we tried X this sprint?"

### Sprint Review + Retrospective
- Facilitate the **Sprint Outcome Review** and **Harmonization Retrospective**
- Ensure emergence events are captured and celebrated
- Track retrospective action items and ensure follow-through
- Share cross-unit patterns with other coaches

### Cross-Unit
- Attend weekly coach sync (30 minutes) to share patterns and challenges
- Contribute observations to the comparison board and **Metrics Monitor** data
- Update the playbook with new learnings from their units

---

## What Coaches Do NOT Do

- **Manage performance** — coaches are not managers, they don't evaluate or rate people
- **Assign work** — units self-organize, coaches facilitate but don't direct
- **Judge individuals** — coaching is about enabling growth, not assessing capability
- **Force adoption** — if someone isn't ready, coaches support them where they are
- **Own the metrics** — metrics belong to the unit, coaches help the unit understand them
- **Be the hero** — the best coaching is invisible; the unit should feel like they did it themselves

---

## Coach Self-Care

Coaching is emotionally demanding. Coaches absorb the anxiety, frustration, and uncertainty of their units while maintaining their own practice.

- Coaches meet weekly with each other for **peer support** — share what's hard, not just what's working
- Each coach has a **fulfillment check-in buddy** outside their coaching relationship
- Coaching load is explicitly accounted for — **20-30% of time** is allocated to coaching, delivery expectations adjusted accordingly
- It's okay to take a sprint off from coaching if fulfillment drops — sustainability matters more than coverage

---

### Organization Extension Point

> **YOUR_ORG:** Adjust coach-to-unit ratios based on your org's facilitation maturity. Orgs with strong existing agile coaching may need fewer dedicated HIO coaches. Orgs with no facilitation tradition may need more coaches or a longer shadow/co-facilitate period. Consider whether coaches should be formally recognized in your performance system and how coaching time is tracked against team capacity.
