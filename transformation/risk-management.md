# Risk Management

## Overview

Six risk categories that span the entire 26-week transformation. Each risk includes triggers (how you know it's happening), impact assessment, mitigation strategies, and monitoring approach. Risks are not problems to eliminate — they are dynamics to manage.

---

## 1. Leadership Loses Patience

The single highest-impact risk. If leadership pulls the plug during the expected dip, the transformation fails regardless of everything else.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Trigger** | Delivery metrics dip in Phase 1, stakeholders complain about slower output, leadership asks "when will this start paying off" before Phase 2 |
| **Impact** | Transformation canceled or defunded, team morale damaged, future change efforts become harder |
| **Mitigation** | Set expectations for the 15% dip **before it happens**. Monthly leadership briefings with the comparison board. Celebrate small wins visibly. Show leading indicators (fulfillment, AI adoption) improving even when lagging indicators (velocity) dip. Secure explicit commitment to the 6-month timeline in Phase 0. |
| **Monitor** | Leadership sentiment in monthly briefings. Questions shifting from "how" to "why are we doing this." Requests for accelerated timelines. Any mention of "pausing" the transformation. |

---

## 2. Pioneer Group Burns Out

Pioneers carry the heaviest cognitive load — learning new ways of working while still delivering, AND being visible examples for the org.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Trigger** | Fulfillment scores drop below 6/10 on Harmony Pulse. Pioneers working overtime. Enthusiasm fades into obligation. People stop volunteering for ceremony facilitation. |
| **Impact** | Pioneer unit underperforms, setting a bad example for expansion. Pioneers become negative advocates. Phase 1 evidence is weak. |
| **Mitigation** | Reduce delivery scope in Phase 1 sprints — learning IS the output. Rotate ceremony facilitation so no one person carries all the weight. Frame struggles as valuable learning data, not failures. Give pioneers explicit permission to say "this isn't working" without consequences. Monitor working hours and enforce boundaries. |
| **Monitor** | Harmony Pulse fulfillment scores weekly. Working hours (self-reported + calendar analysis). Retrospective themes — are people energized or drained? Voluntary participation in extras (showcases, observer sessions). |

---

## 3. AI Tools Aren't Ready

The transformation depends on effective human-AI collaboration. If the tools don't work, the model can't be tested fairly.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Trigger** | Security team blocks tool approval. Tools are unreliable or slow. AI output quality doesn't meet expectations. Integration with existing workflows is harder than expected. |
| **Impact** | Human-AI workflows can't be practiced. AI utilization metrics stay at Level 1. Emergence events don't happen because the AI side is absent. Team loses faith in the model. |
| **Mitigation** | Engage security team in Phase 0 Week 1 — not Week 3. Start with whatever tools ARE available, even if limited. Budget 2-3 days in Week 3 for tool onboarding and troubleshooting. Have fallback plans for each agent — what's the manual equivalent? Designate one person as AI tools point of contact for fast issue resolution. |
| **Monitor** | Tool provisioning status tracker updated daily in Phase 0. AI utilization metrics from Sprint 1 onward. Tool-related frustrations surfaced in retrospectives. Support ticket volume for AI tools. |

---

## 4. Identity Grip Stronger Than Expected

People's attachment to their current role identity ("I'm a backend engineer," "I'm the architect") may resist the fluidity that cognitive functions require.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Trigger** | People refuse to try secondary cognitive functions. Statements like "that's not my job" or "AI can't do what I do." Senior engineers feel their expertise is being devalued. Turf wars over who owns what. |
| **Impact** | Function rotation stalls. Units operate like traditional teams with new labels. Emergence can't happen because people stay in rigid patterns. |
| **Mitigation** | **Never force it** — identity grip responds to invitation, not coercion. Reframe senior expertise as MORE valuable in HIO — their deep knowledge makes AI collaboration richer. Start with low-stakes function experiments (e.g., a senior engineer tries **Fresh-Eyes Observer** on a code review). Coach 1:1 support for individuals struggling with the transition. Share stories from pioneers who found unexpected value in new functions. See [emergence-detection.md](emergence-detection.md) for the identity grip vs. emergence table. |
| **Monitor** | Function rotation participation rate. Language patterns in retrospectives and daily harmony checks. Coach observations from 1:1s. Diversity of functions exercised per person over time. |

---

## 5. Six-Month Timeline Too Aggressive

26 weeks is ambitious for transforming 30 people. Some orgs need more time.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Trigger** | Exit criteria consistently not met at phase boundaries. Units need more than 3 sprints to show improvement. Coach training takes longer than planned. Multiple risks materializing simultaneously. |
| **Impact** | Rushed phases produce shallow adoption. Metrics look okay but practices aren't internalized. Transformation is "complete" on paper but fragile. |
| **Mitigation** | **Phases extend — this is by design, not a failure.** Exit criteria trump dates. If Phase 1 needs 4 sprints instead of 3, it takes 4 sprints. Communicate timeline adjustments to leadership as responsible stewardship, not missed deadlines. Recalculate the overall timeline when any phase extends. Budget 2-4 weeks of buffer in the original plan communication to leadership. |
| **Monitor** | Exit criteria completion rate at each phase boundary. Number of criteria that required extension. Trend of extensions — are they getting shorter (learning) or longer (deeper problems)? |

---

## 6. Rest of Org Becomes Resentful

If the 20+ people NOT in Phase 1 or Phase 2 units feel excluded or judged, the transformation faces internal opposition.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Trigger** | "Us vs them" language. Non-unit members feeling like second-class citizens. Perception that units get special treatment (better tools, less delivery pressure). Sarcasm or passive resistance toward HIO practices. |
| **Impact** | Phase 3 expansion meets active resistance. Org culture fractures. People leave rather than participate. |
| **Mitigation** | **Share everything openly** — metrics, struggles, learnings, not just wins. Use **gradient adoption** — anyone can try cognitive function concepts in their current team. Never compare unit vs. non-unit performance. Ensure non-unit work is valued and recognized equally. Keep the invitation open continuously — people can join units as they form. Frame the transformation as "we're all going through this" not "they're the special group." |
| **Monitor** | Org-wide pulse survey at Weeks 10, 16, and 22. Informal feedback channels. Attrition patterns — are non-unit members leaving? Questions and comments in all-hands forums. |

---

## Risk Review Cadence

| Cadence | Activity | Participants |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| Weekly | Scan all 6 risks against current triggers | HIO coaches |
| Bi-weekly | Review risk status with mitigation updates | Coaches + unit leads |
| Monthly | Full risk review with leadership | Coaches + leadership |
| Phase boundary | Comprehensive risk retrospective | All stakeholders |
